# Story Hook Black-Gold Design Review

## Why this review exists

This style family emerged from a sequence of GPT Image 2 iterations. The useful lesson is not merely "use black and gold." The decisive improvement happened when the task was reframed from a dense technology/course poster into a Douyin Featured thumbnail.

The failed drafts and selected outputs are archived under:

`Josh-video-cover-skill-workspace/design-review/story-hook-black-gold/`

## The turning-point critique

The user gave this direction after seeing a visually polished but overloaded draft:

> 现在这个封面不太像是一个抖音精选的视频封面了。感觉信息是不是有点太多了？人物是不是有点弱，有点太靠后了？我们不需要有那么多信息。主要就是在封面上，核心要告诉用户我们这个封面要传达的内容里面是什么。然后给人的感觉要足够干货，足够有吸引力，符合抖音精选的标准。

This prompt did four important things:

1. Reset the target product from "course poster" to "Douyin Featured thumbnail."
2. Rejected information density instead of asking to beautify it.
3. Promoted the person from a supporting cutout to a foreground trust anchor.
4. Required the image to communicate one core promise and prove that useful content exists.

The black-gold palette was a consequence of the new editorial direction, not the root cause of the improvement.

## What failed

### Dense blue technology drafts

Common symptoms:

- multiple UI cards, workflows, task lists, charts, badges, logos, and code panes compete at the same level
- the person is cleanly rendered but behaves like one more module in a dashboard
- the cover explains the entire syllabus instead of creating one reason to click
- cyan outlines and small labels collapse at thumbnail size
- the result resembles a course landing page, PPT cover, or product dashboard

### Dense black-gold draft

Changing the palette alone did not fix the problem. The overloaded black-gold draft still contained several capability maps, long checklists, charts, and a large notebook. It proved that palette is downstream of hierarchy.

## What won

The selected covers use a four-level hierarchy:

1. **Primary hook:** oversized brush typography, readable before any other element.
2. **Person:** large foreground portrait that creates trust and human presence.
3. **Value promise:** one short gold brush strip explaining what the viewer gets.
4. **Evidence object:** one notebook showing that a reusable method exists.

Background code and charts are atmosphere only. They are not content modules.

## Transferable rules

### One-message rule

The thumbnail should answer one question: "Why should I open this now?" Do not summarize the whole video.

### One-person rule

For a presenter-led knowledge cover, use one large portrait as a foreground anchor. The face and shoulders should not be visually weaker than secondary cards or decorative UI.

### One-evidence rule

Use one concrete proof object: notebook, screenshot, document, product, chart, or physical object. It should support the promise without becoming a second infographic.

### Background capacity

Allow at most two low-contrast atmospheric motifs. Avoid multiple readable UI panels, repeated cards, long task lists, and syllabus-like diagrams.

### Thumbnail contract

At actual feed size:

- the primary hook remains readable
- the person's face remains recognizable
- the value promise is optional but legible
- the evidence object reads as a category, even if its small text is not readable
- all other elements may disappear without harming the message

### Cross-format invariants

The `3:4` and `4:3` versions must preserve:

- the same typography family and material treatment
- the same primary-hook scale relative to the canvas
- the same foreground relationship between person and title
- the same value-promise treatment
- the same evidence-object category
- the same background restraint

Do not preserve pixel positions. Recompose while preserving hierarchy.

## Platform-fit reset

When a draft looks like a dashboard, course poster, PPT cover, or technology template, do not keep polishing individual cards. Reset the direction:

> This does not yet feel like a Douyin Featured thumbnail. There is too much information, the person is visually weak and too far back, and the image is explaining a syllabus instead of one core promise. Rebuild the composition around one strong hook, one large foreground person, one short value promise, and one evidence object. Remove most UI modules and keep only minimal atmospheric technology cues. The result should feel useful, credible, attractive, and immediately readable at feed thumbnail size.

This is a major-direction correction, not a small edit.
